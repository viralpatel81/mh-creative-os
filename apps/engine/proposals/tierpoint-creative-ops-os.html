AI Creative Production OS

Turn creative ops from reactive service into a disciplined system — one typed brief, one policy engine, one closed measurement loop.

This proposal lays out a creative operations platform purpose-built for B2B marketing teams whose throughput is bottlenecked not by talent, but by coordination. The argument is short: every brief that arrives at TierPoint already contains the information needed to produce, gate, publish, and measure the work — but that information is re-typed three times, dropped between tools, and disconnected from the engagement data it generates. The OS encodes the brief once and lets every downstream step read from it.

The result is not "more AI." It is a measurable shift from a creative queue to a creative system — with the same designers, the same agencies, the same Creative Manager, doing the same five jobs, but with the coordination tax removed.

§ 01 · The problem

The structural gap

Creative ops at most B2B teams isn't broken because people aren't trying — it's broken because three layers don't connect.

Intake — mismatched information at the door

Requests arrive in Slack, email, and three different forms. The same brief gets rewritten three times before anyone draws a pixel.

Execution — producers in silos

Designers, copywriters, and outside agencies work to different versions of the truth. QA happens only at delivery — when changing course is most expensive.

Measurement — performance lives elsewhere

Engagement data sits in Looker and GA, disconnected from the creative artifact that produced it. You can't learn from work you can't trace.

The result: a queue, not a system. Throughput depends on a single PM's attention.

Same five jobs. Two different operating modes.

Nothing in this proposal is about doing less work. It is about removing the coordination tax on the work that already happens. Here is what changes, stage by stage.

Before → After
  • Intake. Slack / email / forms; brief rewritten 3× → one typed brief schema; written once, read everywhere.
  • Brief. Prose document; interpretation varies → 11-field artifact with objective, voice pack, policy verdict.
  • Produce. Designers + agencies on parallel tracks → orchestrated handoff; one typed brief as source of truth, assets land in your DAM with lineage.
  • QA. Manual review at the end → eval rubric at every gate; auto-heal on first failure.
  • Measure. Weekly fire drill in Looker → performance.v1 sidecar from your existing analytics; calibration report becomes the input for the next brief.

§ 03 · The system, module by module

The seven modules

The OS is composed of seven independently replaceable modules. Each module reads a typed artifact from the prior step and emits one to the next. Below: what each module does, and the outcome it produces.

Module 3.1 · Intake & briefing

Briefs get written once.

The brief is the contract. Every downstream tool reads it; no one rewrites it.

  • Standardized brief template — a typed schema with 11 required fields: objective, signal, strategy, policy verdict, creative direction, deliverables, voice_pack_id, brand_id, channel, success metric, deadline.
  • Auto-derivation — stakeholder submits a one-line ask; the system drafts a full brief from brand context, prior briefs, and policy. The Creative Manager confirms — not composes.
  • Single source of truth — one typed brief, referenced by ID downstream across every channel and producer. No more "wait, which version?"
Outcome

Stakeholders submit requests once, in a structured format. Briefs stop drifting between revisions.

Module 3.2 · Voice governance

Brand voice as code, not as PDF.

The brand book is the most-cited, least-read document at every company. We make it executable.

  • Typed voice pack — per brand and per audience: tone, style, do/don't rules, lexicon, example responses, banned claims.
  • Drift detection — every drafted piece is scored against the pack. Out-of-tolerance content auto-flags before it reaches a human reviewer.
  • Self-tuning voice — an evolutionary algorithm tests dozens of prompt variants against your labeled examples each cycle and keeps the ones that score best on voice fidelity, claim safety, and clarity simultaneously. Your brand voice gets sharper with use, not staler.
Outcome

Your voice is enforceable, measurable, and evolvable — not a PDF nobody reads.

Module 3.3 · Copy QA at scale

Self-serve copy, on rails.

Most copy requests at a B2B company are routine: a feature blurb, an email subject line, a one-pager headline. They shouldn't burn a Creative Manager's calendar. Four moves make this safe:

  1. Constraints — voice pack as the input. Tone, lexicon, claims policy, audience profile fed into the draft step as hard constraints.
  2. Gate — eval rubric scores. Voice fidelity, on-brand, claim safety, hook strength. Each scored 0–1; thresholds set per channel.
  3. Recovery — auto-heal once. On failure, the system patches the draft against the failing rubric and retries — exactly once, with reasoning logged.
  4. Human gate — escalation queue. Anything that still fails routes to the Creative Manager with the rubric trace attached. No blind reviews.
Outcome

Teams self-serve copy responsibly. The Creative Manager is the quality gate, not the bottleneck.

Module 3.4 · Designer + agency workflow

Designers and agencies, in the system.

Visuals and complex layouts are produced by your internal designers and outside partners. We don't replace them — we make them legible.

  • Typed brief as the handoff artifact — one structured brief (audience, voice pack, deliverable specs, deadline) becomes the input every producer reads, whether they work in Figma, Adobe CC, or off-platform. Tool-specific surfaces (Figma plugin, Adobe panel) are scoped during configuration based on your designers' workflows.
  • Capacity + timelines — every brief produces a Monday/Asana ticket with owner, due date, and dependency on upstream gates. No spreadsheets.
  • Review gates — drafts route to Brand & Creative leadership via the same policy engine that gates copy. One approval surface, not five.
  • DAM lineage — final assets push into your DAM tagged with brief ID, voice pack ID, and approval history. Every asset traceable to its origin.
Outcome

Internal designers, external agencies, and freelancers stop being a coordination tax. They become legible, accountable production capacity.

Module 3.5 · Review gates & policy

Every output passes one policy engine.

Copy or asset, internal or agency — same three-lane verdict. Reasons are logged. Appeals are auditable.

Eval rubric scores attach to every verdict. Failing drafts auto-heal once before escalating. Every approve / reject logged with reason.

Outcome

One policy engine governs every creative output — copy, visual, internal, agency — with full reason trace and appeal history.

Module 3.6 · Audit & lineage

Every asset, traceable to its origin.

Five typed JSON Schemas form a chain. Pick any asset; walk backwards to the brief, the voice pack, the policy verdict, the reviewer.

voice_pack.v1  →  brief.v1  →  forecast.v1  →  run_result.v1  →  performance.v1
  • Who made this — brief author, voice pack version, draft model, reviewer, approval timestamp.
  • Why — the source signal, the strategy, the policy verdict and its reasons.
  • What happened — published surface, engagement metrics, calibration delta vs forecast.
Outcome

Answer "who made this, why, and what happened" for any creative output ever produced. Defensible to legal, repeatable for the next campaign.

Module 3.7 · Measurement loop

The system reports on itself.

Three commands produce the audit + calibration the Creative Manager needs to shape the next brief.

  • metrics adapt — takes run_result.v1 plus a sidecar of realized engagement numbers (brought in from whatever analytics surface your team uses) and emits performance.v1, the canonical post-campaign artifact.
  • metrics calibrate — reads performance.v1 against the original forecast.v1 and produces an alignment report: which predicted outcomes matched, which missed, and why.
  • metrics study — pipeline-wide retrospective combining forecast accuracy, voice consistency, and creative effectiveness into a single readable artifact.
Outcome

Reports stop being weekly fire drills. The system produces the audit trail; the Creative Manager reads the calibration and shapes the next brief. Human-in-the-strategy, system-in-the-mechanics.

Clear lines. The OS doesn't pretend to render hero film. It does make sure every hero film traces back to a brief, a voice pack, and a measurable outcome.

  • Social posts (organic LinkedIn, X, threads)
  • Blog drafts and long-form outlines
  • Sales-enablement headlines and one-pager copy
  • Outreach and reply drafts
  • Typographic sales cards (card lab)

Orchestrated

Briefed, tracked, and QA'd by the OS — produced by your team or partners. The OS provides the brief, the gate, and the lineage; your designers and agencies provide the craft.

  • Visual ads and paid creative
  • Complex multi-layer layouts and brand campaigns
