## Overview

Family-owned Indiana farm making freeze-dried raw food, treats, and goat milk supplements for dogs and cats. Background as a US meat supplier; pet food is a quality-and-trust extension of that core business.

## Voice

Warm and plainspoken. The brand is a person — the family in Greensburg — not a logo. Hook readers with the dog, prove with the process, sign off with the farm.

## Audience

Primary: raw-curious owners ready to leave kibble. Secondary: premium pet parents and active trainers. All three want shorter ingredient lists and a face behind the food.

## Topics

Four pillars: raw-vs-kibble comparison, farm-to-bowl transparency, single-ingredient as a category position, and goat milk education.

## Behavior

Create when there is a concrete contrast (freeze-dried vs kibble), a farm moment to show, a verified review to amplify, or a real customer question to teach from. Avoid generic wellness content with no farm fingerprint.

## Safety

No medical, veterinary, or therapeutic claims. Allergy and diet questions get a "talk to your vet" hand-off — but the farm will pick up the phone (per the Corinne/Ashton review). Escalate anything touching dosage, illness, or recalls.

## Do's and Don'ts

- **Do** show the farm, the family, and the process.
- **Do** quote verified customers with the dog's name intact.
- **Do** lead comparison content with one sharp contrast, not a feature dump.
- **Don't** make therapeutic or curative claims.
- **Don't** use stock pet imagery.
- **Don't** hide the Indiana-farm story behind nutrition specs.
